# Technical writing

Lead with the outcome and explain the approach in connected, concise prose. Define domain terms when needed, keep terminology consistent, and give exact paths, units, commands, and examples. Explain rationale for tradeoffs, non-obvious behavior, and departures from established patterns.

Use active voice and plain words. Remove throat-clearing, marketing claims, repeated summaries, generic praise, and decorative structure. Preserve real uncertainty rather than replacing it with false confidence. A short complete example is more useful than repetitive examples in many languages.

Document public behavior, inputs/outputs, error conditions, units, guarantees, and prerequisites. Comments explain why a decision exists. Reports distinguish observed evidence from inference, pending checks, and assumptions. Do not expose workflow implementation detail in product copy unless it helps the product's user make a decision.
